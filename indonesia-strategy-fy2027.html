Section 02
The Challenge

Three structural headwinds facing the Indonesia BU that the strategy must address.

Low CAPEX Willingness

Only 10–15% of the Indonesian solar market is willing to fund their own solar installation (CAPEX model). The majority of factory owners and offtakers are reluctant to put up upfront capital — making C&I deal flow inherently constrained.

GP Margin Under Pressure

Current AOP GP margin is 14.7%, flagged by Finance as too low. The proposed strategy to reduce GP% further to win deals creates a direct tension that requires a deliberate management decision and trade-off approval.

PPA Model Frozen

The PPA model, which typically enables broader market access, has been frozen for general C&I deployment. This limits addressable market to the 10–15% CAPEX-willing segment unless financing solutions are introduced.

💡

Strategic Insight

The constraints above are addressable — but only if we deliberately expand the CAPEX-willing pool through bank financing partnerships, and accept a short-term GP trade-off in exchange for higher win rates and market share. This is the core management decision.

Section 03
Our Strategic Response — C&I

Four integrated pillars to win the addressable CAPEX market and unlock new demand.

Pillar 01

Win the CAPEX Market with Better ROI

  • Reduce EPC GP% to make offtaker ROI compelling
  • Target payback period <5 years for clients
  • Post-payback: client enjoys ~15 years of free savings
  • Educate PPA-inclined clients on CAPEX attractiveness
Pillar 02

Build a Subcontractor Pool

  • Develop dedicated subcon network in Indonesia
  • Sign Blanket EPC Agreements upfront
  • Issue Work Orders per project under master contract
  • Reduce cost, speed up mobilisation, standardise terms
Pillar 03

Subcon GP Optimisation

  • Option A: Reduce subcon GP% → offset with better payment terms
  • Option B: Increase subcon GP% → attract better quality subcons → higher project delivery capability
  • Both options target overall cost reduction
Pillar 04

Bank Financing Partnerships

  • Engage local Indonesian banks for solar financing packages
  • Introduce financing options to CAPEX-hesitant clients
  • Co-market with banks to reach more factories
  • Client value: ~20% electricity savings over 25 years
⚖️

The GP Trade-Off — A Deliberate Choice

Reducing EPC GP% will push margin lower in the short term. However, this is a calculated move: a higher win rate on CAPEX deals grows total project volume, recovers absolute GP in aggregate, and builds long-term market presence. Management alignment on an acceptable GP floor is required before execution.

Section 05
Utility Strategy

Indonesia has utility-scale opportunity — but requires focused resourcing and a structured development approach.

Start with Smaller Projects

Focus on smaller-scale utility projects first to build track record, understand regulatory landscape, and reduce DEVEX risk. A DEVEX checklist will be developed to itemise all required pre-development expenditures per project.

Dedicated Utility BD Hire

Hire 1 dedicated Utility BD person specifically for utility project development and pipeline management. Current BD team is C&I-focused and cannot carry utility development responsibilities concurrently.

📋

DEVEX Checklist — To Be Developed

A detailed DEVEX (Development Expenditure) checklist needs to be compiled for each utility project — covering land agreements, grid interconnection, environmental permits, PLN approvals, and related pre-construction costs. This will inform go/no-go decisions and capital allocation.
