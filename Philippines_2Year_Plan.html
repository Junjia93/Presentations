🔄 Revenue Model — How Solarvest Earns
Step 1 — Acquire
Identify greenfield/brownfield sites. Secure land rights, grid interconnection study, preliminary permits. Cost: USD 30/kWp in FY27.
Step 2 — Develop to RTB
Full permits, EIA clearance, grid connection approval, PPA/offtake agreement, bankable project documents. Additional USD 20/kWp in FY28.
Step 3 — Flip to Investor
Sell RTB project rights to a fund/investor at premium to development cost. Earn development fee (~5–8% of total project value).
Step 4 — Back-in EPC
As condition of the flip, Solarvest is awarded the EPC contract. Construction begins, generating core EPC revenue stream.

La Mesa Dam, managed by MWSS (Metropolitan Waterworks and Sewerage System), has an approximate water surface that can accommodate ~1 GWp of floating photovoltaic solar. Bayanwest's Paul has an existing MWSS contact. The project would be structured as a Public Private Partnership (PPP) — Solarvest leads the bid with a new SPV (Solarvest majority, Bayanwest potential co-investor). USD 500K is required upfront to formally engage and secure preferential position before a competitive PPP process is launched.

Risk Register
Key risks across all three Philippines business streams with mitigation actions
# Stream Risk Likelihood Impact Rating Mitigation
R1 JV JVco legal structure delay — shareholder agreement takes longer than expected, delaying JVco registration and Q2/Q3 operations Medium High High Engage Philippines corporate legal counsel immediately. Target JVco registration by end of June 2026. Flag to MD as critical path item.
R2 JV Economic vs. legal interest misalignment — Bayanwest (60% legal) challenges Solarvest's 65% economic interest at a later stage Medium High High Ensure SHA explicitly documents economic interest split, dividend waterfall, and technical service fees. Paul + MD alignment confirmed at April 21 meeting is a positive signal.
R3 JV Project engineering capacity overload — 4 engineers insufficient at full PHP 875M volume (flagged in Bayanwest's own plan) High Medium High Pre-approve headcount expansion plan. Target hiring 2 additional project engineers by Q3 FY27 ahead of scale-up. Solarvest can second engineers from Malaysia/Taiwan as interim support.
R4 SPV Grid interconnection queue delays — NGCP (National Grid Corporation Philippines) has known backlogs for large solar projects High High High Prioritise projects with existing grid connection proximity. Engage NGCP early with multiple sites. Consider battery storage component to fast-track approvals. Accept FY28 RTB milestone may slip to FY29 for some projects.
R5 SPV 75 MWp acquisition pipeline quality — securing viable sites at USD 30/kWp proves difficult; projects have encumbrances or poor solar resource Medium Medium Medium Hire Philippines-based project development manager with NGCP/DOE network. Prioritise brownfield sites (industrial, former mining). Target 100 MWp pipeline to filter down to 75 MWp quality projects.
R6 La Mesa PPP timeline slippage — government processes slow; La Mesa Dam PPP takes 2–3 years instead of 1 year, pushing revenue to FY29/FY30 High Medium Medium La Mesa is NOT in base revenue projection — treat as pure upside. USD 500K is a positioning investment, not a committed project. Exit criteria: if no MOU with MWSS by Q4 FY27, reassess commitment.
R7 La Mesa Environmental / water source conflict — La Mesa Dam is a water supply reservoir; floating solar may face opposition from environmental groups or NWRB Medium High High Commission environmental pre-feasibility study as part of initial engagement. Engage MWSS, NWRB, DENR simultaneously. Frame floating solar as water evaporation reduction benefit (not just power generation).
R8 All FX exposure (PHP/USD/MYR three-way) — PHP depreciation reduces RM-equivalent revenue; USD cost inflation on SPV acquisitions Medium Medium Medium Negotiate USD-denominated contracts where possible for utility projects. JV C&I contracts in PHP — natural hedge via PHP costs. Request Group Treasury guidance on hedging policy for overseas markets.
R9 All Key man risk — Paul (Bayanwest) — entire Philippines government/utility network depends on Paul's personal relationships Low High Medium Ensure Solarvest staff are co-present at all key meetings to build parallel relationships. JVco agreement should include non-compete/exit provisions. Develop secondary contacts within Bayanwest team.

⚠️ Project Engineer Capacity Risk
4 engineers × 1,040 projects = 260 projects/engineer/year. Even with residential being small (5 kWp), the 40 C&I projects at 500 kWp each = 10 large projects per engineer simultaneously. Recommend pre-approving hire of 2 additional engineers before PHP 875M run-rate is hit. Solarvest Malaysia can second 1–2 engineers as bridge support.
⚡ Sales Pipeline Volume
4 sales staff (1 manager + 3 officers) need to generate and manage 3,600 leads to close 1,040 projects. That is 900 leads per sales person. Highly dependent on Bayanwest's existing network and referral pipeline — not a cold-start problem if Bayanwest already has C&I relationships in market.
